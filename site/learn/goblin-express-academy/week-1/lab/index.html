The Boss Delivery

Turn the shift loop into a working week that someone else can play from start to finish, with harder days, a delivery for Madam Grizzletooth and a verdict at the end. Then watch someone play it.

00 · Shift memo

A shift is not a week

To:Courier Snag's handler (you) From:Madam Grizzletooth, Proprietor Re:The boss delivery

Snag can work a shift. Lovely. A business runs on weeks: days that get busier, and at the end of it, one delivery for me personally. What's in the parcel is between you and Claude. Handle it with care.

Then show the week to someone who has never seen it, and say nothing while they play. If they need you to explain something, the game isn't finished.

— G.

By the end of this lab you will

  • Design a bigger feature with Claude's questions, and check its numbers before you build it.
  • Build it in playable slices, with a quick test for each.
  • Take one branch: restyle the Depot from your own sketch, or save the week in the browser.
  • Watch someone else play your game without helping, and turn what you saw into fixes.

This lab picks up where Day 4 left off, with the job board, tips and a shift that can end early. If you did Day 4's shop side quest, the week will use it. If not, Claude will ask what to do about upgrades.

01 · Plan the week · 25 min

The interview, again

A week is a bigger design than a shift, with more choices that only you can make. Day 4's interview (§11) and its math check (§13) are made for exactly this.

1. Start fresh

Terminal or PowerShell
cd goblin-express
claude

If Claude Code is still open, type /clear instead. Stay in ⏸ manual mode on for the planning.

2. Ask for the questions

Prompt
This is the boss delivery: turning the shift loop into a working week that someone else can sit down and play from start to finish. I want:

- shifts that get harder as the week goes on
- a boss delivery to end the week: one special job from Madam Grizzletooth, who owns Goblin Express
- an end-of-week screen that sums up how the week went

Read DESIGN.md and the game first. Then, before you change anything, ask me the design questions you need answered. Give each one a suggested answer, so I can just agree where I don't mind.

In our test run, Claude asked sixteen questions: how long the week is, what gets harder, who Madam Grizzletooth is, what's in her parcel, what the end screen shows. Two of them:

From Claude
1. How many shifts in a week?
   Suggested: five shifts, Monday to Friday, then the boss delivery.
   With 60–90 second shifts and the shop screens in between, that
   makes a run of about 8 minutes.

10. What's special about the job?
   Suggested: it's one parcel, "her dentures, freshly gilded". It waits
   on the shelf farthest from a door, and Snag can't dash while
   carrying it. This one rule changes how the delivery plays and is
   easy to see.

3. Answer with the playtester in mind

Someone else will play this week at the end of the lab, so every minute of it is a minute of their time. That's a design call, and it's yours:

Prompt
[your answers, for example: All your suggestions, except: make the week three days, Monday to Wednesday, so a playtester can finish it in about five minutes.]

Add the design we agreed to DESIGN.md, in plain words a designer could check: the rules, the starting numbers, and what we're leaving out for now. Commit it on main, and don't build anything yet.

Claude will list the details it filled in on its own, as it did on Day 4. Read them.

4. Do the math on the week

Prompt
Using the numbers in DESIGN.md, work out how a careful player and a sloppy one would do on each day and on the boss delivery. Show it in a small table, with the verdict each one ends up with. Tell me if anything in DESIGN.md looks off. Don't change any code.
From Claude
Player   Monday  Tuesday  Wednesday  Boss delivery  Teeth earned
Careful  ≈ 49    ≈ 60     ≈ 71       30, 5 stars    ~198–210
Sloppy   ≈ 9     ≈ 8      ≈ 5        9, 3 stars     ~22–31

When we ran it, the math found the week's biggest flaw before a line of it was built: the boss delivery couldn't change anyone's verdict. A careful player had the top title before the boss delivery even started, and a sloppy one couldn't climb out of the bottom whatever happened. It also found that the 30-second timer on the boss delivery would never run out. Decide what to change, and say so:

Example
Make the boss bonus 80 teeth, so the last delivery can change your title, and give it 15 seconds with no Overtime. The rest is intended: say so in DESIGN.md where it matters, and fill in the small gaps. Update DESIGN.md and commit it on main. Don't build anything yet.

02 · Build the week · 50 min

Three slices to Wednesday

The same rhythm as Day 4: plan the slices, then build, play and commit each one before the next.

1. Plan the slices

Prompt
Read DESIGN.md and split the week into about three slices I can play one at a time, smallest first. For each slice, tell me what I'll see and the quickest way to test it. Don't change any code yet.

Expect something like this: the harder days first, then the end-of-week screen, then the boss delivery. Most of the quick tests come down to one temporary change: a much shorter shift in the tuning file, so you can get through a whole week in under a minute. That's §12 again. Put the number back before each commit.

2. Build, play, commit

Switch to accept edits (Shift + Tab until the status bar shows ⏵⏵ accept edits on; on Windows, Alt + M if that does nothing). Then, for each slice:

Prompt
Build the next slice, then stop so I can test it.
Prompt
Commit this slice.

Play each slice against DESIGN.md. A few things worth checking:

  • Harder days: does the top bar name the day? Does a delivery that was fine on Monday rate lower later in the week?
  • The end of the week: do the totals add up? Mash Space as the screen appears: it shouldn't skip. Does a new week start clean?
  • The boss delivery: does your special rule work, whatever you chose? What happens when the time runs out?

In our run, Claude checked the tuning file for leftover test values before every commit, without being asked. That's worth noticing, and still worth checking yourself.

03 · Pick a branch · 35 min

Art or code

Pick the one closer to your day job, or the one further from it. Either way, you're directing Claude the same way: say what you want, look at the result, and say what's off.

Art branch: dress the Depot

Right now the Depot is grey boxes. Make a mood sketch of how it should look. A photo of a paper sketch is fine, and so is ten minutes in any paint program. Show the colors you want, with a note on each thing, the way you'd brief an artist. The game brief's direction is "grimy but warm": mossy greens, rust and sooty purples, with parcels in warning-label yellow.

Save it inside your game folder, in a new folder called art, so it stays with the project. Then drag the image onto the Claude Code window, as you did with screenshots on Day 2, and finish the prompt:

Prompt
[your sketch] This is how I want the Depot to look. Restyle the floor, walls, crates, shelves, doors and Snag to match it. Keep every color in one place in the data folder so I can tweak them by hand, and don't change how the game plays. Then stop and tell me what to look at.

Claude can't see the game, so it can't judge the result. You can. Play for a minute, take a screenshot of whatever's off, save it in art, and drag it in:

Example
[screenshot] The waiting parcels get lost on the wooden shelves, especially when they're dimmed while Snag is carrying. My sketch says parcels must pop against everything. Fix that, and don't change anything else.

When it looks right:

Prompt
Commit this on main, including my sketch in art/.

Code branch: save the week

Right now, closing the tab loses the week. Saving it sounds like one feature, but it's a dozen decisions. Let Claude ask them:

Prompt
I want the week saved in the browser, so closing the tab on Tuesday and coming back picks up on Tuesday. Read DESIGN.md first. Then, before you change anything, ask me the design questions you need answered, with a suggested answer for each.

In our run, it asked the questions a careful programmer would: when the game saves, what happens to a save from an older version of the game, what to do in a private window that won't save at all, and whether closing the tab partway through a bad shift lets a player dodge being sent home. Answer, then build it in slices:

Prompt
[your answers, for example: All your suggestions, except 2: closing the tab partway through a shift counts as being sent home early.]

Update DESIGN.md and commit it on main. Then build the first slice and stop so I can test it.

Then build, test and commit the rest of the slices as before. To test a save, reload the page. Once saving works, your own testing will pick up old weeks too, so ask Claude how to start a fresh one.

In our run, Claude suggested testing in Chrome, since it wasn't sure Safari keeps saved data for a page opened straight from a file. If your playtester will use Safari, check it there too.

04 · Outside playtest · 35 min

Somebody else's hands

You know where every parcel is and what every number means. Your playtester doesn't, and that's the point. Anyone who hasn't seen the game counts: a colleague, a friend, someone you live with. They don't need to play games.

1. Get the game to them

The easiest way is to sit them at your computer. If they're somewhere else, send the game folder. Because of Day 3's rule, they don't need to install anything: they unzip it and double-click index.html.

In Finder, Control-click the goblin-express folder and choose Compress "goblin-express". A .zip file appears next to it.

In File Explorer, right-click the goblin-express folder and choose Compress to ZIP file (on some versions, Compress to, then ZIP File). On Windows 10, choose Send to, then Compressed (zipped) folder.

Share the zip as a link from a file-sharing service rather than an email attachment: email services such as Gmail block zip files that contain scripts. If they're remote, ask them to share their screen on a video call while they play.

2. Watch, and say nothing

Tell them it's a delivery game and you'd like them to play a whole week, then stop talking. Write down what they do and say: where they hesitate, what keys they press, what they read and what they skip, when they laugh. Every time you want to explain something, write that down too. That's a note about the game, not about them.

§14

Company policyWatch, don't help. When someone playtests your game, say nothing and take notes. Every time you'd have explained something, the game needs to.

3. Sort the notes

Prompt
I watched someone play the whole week without helping. My notes:

[paste your notes]

Sort these into bugs, design problems and polish. For each one, say what you'd change and how big a change it is. Then pick the three that matter most for a new player. Don't change anything yet.

We tried it with notes like these:

Example
- Skimmed the title screen, pressed Space, then asked "what do I do?" Found the parcels after about 10 seconds.
- Pressed E to pick up a parcel, several times.
- Didn't notice the ✗ marks at the top. Got sent home on Tuesday and said "wait, why?"
- Didn't buy anything until the third shop. Hadn't noticed the number keys.
- Boss card: "the top bar still says Wednesday and Time: 0. Is it broken?"
- Boss delivery: the timer went red almost straight away, and they thought they'd already failed.
- Refreshed the page by accident on Wednesday. Welcome back said they'd walked out and been sent home. Annoyed.
- Laughed at the dentures line and at Grizzletooth's verdict.

Claude checked every note against the code before sorting it. It found that the boss card really was a bug, recognized the refresh as the save design working as agreed (and offered two ways to soften it), and put these first:

From Claude
1. The opening. Every new player hits it in the first ten seconds,
   and it sets how the rest of the session feels.
2. The strike warning. A punishment the player doesn't understand
   feels unfair, not difficult.
3. The shop keys. The upgrades are the game's whole progression
   system, and this player missed them for two of three shops.
§15

Company policySort before you fix. Playtest notes are a pile, not a plan. Have Claude sort them into bugs, design problems and polish, check each one against the code, and pick the few that matter most. Fix those first.

4. Fix the top of the list

Say which ones to fix, and keep the design file in step:

Example
Go with your top three, plus 1 and 4 since they're tiny. Update DESIGN.md and commit it on main first. Then fix them one at a time, and stop after the first so I can test it.

Then, after you've tested each fix:

Prompt
Commit this on main, then do the next one and stop so I can test it.

If you can, show the fixed game to someone new. A playtester only sees a game for the first time once.

05 · Check and clock out · 10 min

Back to the book

A design, three slices, a branch and a round of fixes: time to check the game and DESIGN.md still agree. As on Day 4, switch to ⏸ manual mode on, start fresh, and ask:

Prompt
/clear
Prompt
Compare the game with DESIGN.md. List anything that works differently from what the design says, and anything the game does that the design doesn't mention. Don't change anything yet.

Settle each difference: fix the game, or update the design. In our run, settling them turned up one more real bug, which is a good reason not to skip this step.

Prompt
Commit any remaining changes on main, then show me today's commits, one line each.

Incident reports

Claude put a commit on a new branch no "on main" in a fresh session

It happened in our run, in the session that did the restyle. Week 1 keeps everything on main:

Prompt
Put that commit on main and delete the other branch.
Claude can't open your sketch or screenshot the file is outside the game folder

Claude needs your permission to read files outside the folder you started it in. Allow it when asked, or move the image into the game's art folder and drag it in from there.

The game opens on an old week your save is working

Code branch only. Start a fresh week the way your design says, or ask:

Prompt
How do I start a fresh week while I'm testing?
A whole week goes by in seconds a test shortcut got committed
Prompt
Check the tuning file for any values we changed for testing, put them back to what DESIGN.md says, and commit.
Blank screen after a slice an error in the console

Same fix as Day 3: open the browser console, copy the red lines and paste them in. Day 3, step 4 has the shortcuts for each browser.

Debrief questions

  • What did your playtester do that you never would have?

★ Side quest · optional · ~30 min

The other branch

Take the branch you skipped. If you saved the week, the art branch is a gentle way to try directing visuals. If you restyled the Depot, the code branch shows how many decisions hide inside "just save it".

Or take the playtest to your own game. Watch someone play it without helping, then hand Claude your notes:

Prompt
I watched someone play my game without helping. My notes:

[paste your notes]

Read the game first. Then sort these into bugs, design problems and polish, say what you'd change for each, and pick the three that matter most for a new player. Don't change anything yet.
