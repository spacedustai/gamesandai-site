TWO MINUTE SPORTS

Coach the next play.

You coach the bright orange Embers. Choose an offensive play, see the formation, the quarterback snaps when ready. Players handle the action. When Comets have possession, choose defense; their computer coach snaps.

After each whistle, players recover and can gather between calls. An early call sends available players directly to their formation. Open Subs to send an eligible Embers reserve; the outgoing player must leave before the replacement enters. Names, jerseys and ratings change with the player. This scrimmage has a local two-way roster; Comets keep their lineup.

The result and substitution window is untimed. Call next play starts the 20-second play clock. The first clock starts when you open Choose a play, never simply from loading the page. It keeps running through selection, exchanges and lineup. Required offensive recovery, ball spotting and substitutions finish physically. Complete long recoveries or exchanges in the untimed window before calling. Snap waits for a legally set offense, the ball/current center, completed offensive exchanges and an official safely clear of the center. Defensive gatherings and substitutions do not hold the offense. Defenders keep their actual positions and motion. A defender in or beyond the neutral zone at the snap draws offside; play continues, then the offensive coach may accept the five-yard penalty or keep the play. Impending contact or an unobstructed moving rusher is whistled dead before the snap.

Call next play starts twenty seconds, including selection and formation travel. Expiry brings delay of game. You have one timeout for this scrimmage; resume it when ready. Audibles remain available during formation with two preset alternatives and do not reset the clock.

Drag the field to orbit. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Use the camera menu for a lower sideline, a close field view, or a tilted stadium overview. Tap a player or open Roster to inspect actual abilities.

The sideline camera follows the ball and adjusts its framing during play. Each captured play gets a short, skippable replay before its concise result appears in the top scoreboard. Pending offside shows the actual play first, then Accept/Decline. Replay can show the last recorded play again. Players keep recovering underneath replay, and a running play clock keeps running.

Development scrimmage: touchdowns are six points and start a new drive at the other team's 25. Special teams, full matches, saving, and online play come later. The two-minute game-clock policy remains undecided; only the play clock runs. Help does not pause it.
